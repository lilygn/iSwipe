<h3 style="font-weight: normal"> 


<h2>Looking for Graduate Students:<br></h2>
<h3>The <A HREF="https://acecenter.grainger.illinois.edu/">SRC/DARPA JUMP 2.0 ACE Center for Evolvable Computing</A> has started in January 2023. This 5-year 21-Principal Investigator project is focused on transforming distributed computing, substantially improving its energy efficiency and performance. See the <A HREF="https://acecenter.grainger.illinois.edu/news/52532">Official Announcement</A>, as well as the SRC announcements (<A HREF="https://www.src.org/newsroom/press-release/2023/1037/">here</A> and <A HREF="https://twitter.com/srcorg/status/1610637176798068736?s=20&t=woR80Se0_XlUgPjO4CvwPw">here</A>) and the
<A HREF="https://www.darpa.mil/news-events/2023-01-04">DARPA announcement</A>. I am looking for graduate students who want to get involved in the Center. It is a great opportunity for individuals who are looking to learn and perform research on cross-disciplinary issues in computing systems, interact with students and senior researchers across the community, and have a close collaboration with computer industry leaders. 
If interested, please send an email with your CV to torrella@illinois.edu.<br>

<h2>JOSEP TORRELLAS BIO:</h2>
<h3 style="font-weight: normal"> 

Josep Torrellas is the Saburo Muroga Professor of <A HREF="https://cs.illinois.edu">Computer Science at the University of Illinois at Urbana-Champaign (UIUC)</a>. He is the 
Director of the <A HREF="https://acecenter.grainger.illinois.edu/">SRC/DARPA JUMP 2.0 ACE Center for Evolvable Computing</A>, past Co-Leader of an Intel Strategic Research Alliance (ISRA) on Computer Security, and past Director of the 
<A HREF="http://i2pc.cs.illinois.edu">Illinois-Intel Parallelism Center (I2PC)</a>.

<br>
<p>
<p>

Torrellas has made contributions to shared-memory multiprocessor architectures and thread-level speculation (TLS) over more than thirty years. He was the first to apply TLS ideas to parallel architectures and programs: He used speculative multithreading to eliminate stalling due to synchronization (Speculative Synchronization), identify and debug data races (ReEnact), monitor memory accesses (I-Watcher), provide fault tolerance (ReVive), and inexpensively enforce sequential consistency (BulkSC). Some of these ideas impacted IBM's Blue Gene and other commercial machines. Torrellas was part of the design team of <A HREF="https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/PERCS">IBMâs PERCS Multiprocessor</a>, an architecture for productivity. The team considered some of his TLS mechanisms, as well as processing-in-memory features inspired by his <A HREF="http://iacoma.cs.uiuc.edu/flexram/index.html">FlexRAM Intelligent Memory</a> project.

<br>
<p>

Using mechanisms inspired by TLS, Torrellas developed the <A HREF="http://iacoma.cs.uiuc.edu/iacoma-papers/bulkmulticore.pdf">Bulk Architecture</a>, an out-of-the-box scalable shared-memory multiprocessor for programmability that was influential within Intel as part of the <A HREF="http://iacoma.cs.uiuc.edu/iacoma-papers/Illinois_parallelism_book.pdf">Illinois-Intel Parallelism Center</a>. Torrellas also applied TLS ideas to develop Record and deterministic Replay (RnR) architectures and, together with Intel, he built <A HREF="http://iacoma.cs.uiuc.edu/iacoma-papers/isca13_1.pdf">QuickRec, the first RnR hardware prototype</a>, which used FPGAs to extend Intelâs cores and caches for RnR.

<br>
<p>

Torrellas has made contributions to multiprocessor architectures, NUMA organizations, cache coherence protocols, hardware synchronization, and prefetching schemes. In recognition, the government made his I-ACOMA multiprocessor one of the <A HREF="https://www.eurekalert.org/pub_releases/1996-10/NSF-NfSN-251096.php">Ten Design-Point Studies</a> funded nationwide to attain Petaflop machines. He was involved in the <A HREF="https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Stanford_DASH">Stanford DASH</a> and <A HREF="https://dl.acm.org/doi/abs/10.1145/165123.165157">Illinois Cedar</a> experimental multiprocessors. His early work on <A HREF="http://iacoma.cs.uiuc.edu/iacoma-papers/false_sharing.pdf">false sharing</a> and cache behavior of OS and commercial workloads influenced compiler writers and architects of early parallel machines, such as the Silicon Graphics Origin2000. Torrellas was also co-leader of the <A HREF="http://iacoma.cs.uiuc.edu/iacoma-papers/hpca13_1.pdf">Intel Runnemede Extreme-Scale multiprocessor</a>, a machine designed for energy efficiency that impacted Intelâs high-end architectures. 

<br>
<p>

Torrellas received the 2021 IEEE Computer Society <A HREF="https://www.computer.org/volunteering/awards/goode">Harry H. Goode Memorial Award</a> âfor contributions to energy efficient and programmable shared-memory multiprocessor architecturesâ, the 2017 UIUC <A HREF="https://cs.illinois.edu/news/torrellas-earns-campus-wide-student-mentoring-honor">Campus Award for Excellence in Graduate Student Mentoring</a>, and the 2015 IEEE Computer Society <A HREF="https://www.computer.org/volunteering/awards/technical-achievement">Technical Achievement Award</a> âfor <A HREF="https://www.computer.org/profiles/josep-torrellas">pioneering contributions to shared-memory multiprocessor architectures and thread-level speculation</a>â. He has received a Google Faculty Research Award, IBM Partnership Awards, Intel Research Council Awards, and an NSF Young Investigator Award. He was a Willett Faculty Scholar at Illinois (2002-9). He is a Fellow of IEEE, ACM, and <A HREF="https://www.aaas.org/news/2016-aaas-fellows-honored-advancing-science-serve-society">AAAS</a>. He has received many Best Paper Awards and a High-Impact Paper Award.

<br>
<p>

Torrellas has mentored generations of computer architects. Of his 48 Ph.D. graduates, over a dozen are faculty at top US academic institutions, including MartÃ­nez (Cornell), Solihin (UCF), Huang (Rochester), Prvulovic (Gatech), Renau (UCSC), Tuck (NCSU), Ceze (Washington), Teodorescu (OSU), Ahn (Pittsburgh), Muzahid (Texas A&M), Karpuzcu (Minnesota), Qian (Purdue), Honarmand (Stony Brook), Yan (MIT), and Skarlatos (CMU).

<br>
<p>

Torrellas has served as the Chair of the <A HREF="http://ieeetcca.org">IEEE CS Technical Committee on Computer Architecture (TCCA)</a> (2018-22). He has also served in the Board of Directors of the <A HREF="https://cra.org">Computing Research Association (CRA)</a> (2016-19), has been a Council Member of CRAâs <A HREF="https://cra.org/ccc/">Computing Community Consortium (CCC)</a> (2011-14), and has served as a Member of the <A HREF="https://www.nationalacademies.org/board/board-on-army-research-and-development">U.S. National Academies Board on Army Research and Development</a> (2018-23). He currently serves in the <A HREF="https://irds.ieee.org">International Roadmap for Devices and Systems (IRDS)</a>. He has co-organized many <A HREF="http://iacoma.cs.uiuc.edu/iacoma-papers/inter18.pdf">funding and visioning workshops</a>. His workshops on <A HREF="http://iacoma.cs.uiuc.edu/iacoma-papers/ACAR1_REPORT.pdf">Advancing Computer Architecture Research</a> helped usher NSFâs XPS Program.

<br>
<p>

Prior to being at UIUC, Torrellas received a Ph.D. from Stanford University.
